# New /deepthink: Exploration Tool Design

## First Principles

What is exploration actually?
- Not answering questions - **finding the right questions**
- Not evaluating options - **discovering what options exist**
- Not confirming hypotheses - **generating hypotheses worth testing**
- Starts with "I don't know what I don't know"

## Fundamental Truths About Exploration

1. You must see the territory before you can navigate it - mapping comes first
2. Your initial framing is probably wrong - you need perspective shifts
3. The interesting stuff is in the edges and connections, not the obvious centers
4. You need to hold contradictions without resolving them prematurely
5. What you're NOT seeing is often more important than what you are

## Phase Structure

### Phase 1: MAP
Systems thinking + visual output. "What exists? What connects?"

Before anything else, map what exists. Components, relationships, feedback loops. Visual output is crucial - spatial representation lets you see the whole territory at once. This isn't analysis yet, just "what's here?"

### Phase 2: INVERT
Pre-mortem + assumption surfacing. "How could this fail? What am I assuming?"

Attack your own map. Inversion surfaces hidden constraints and assumptions. Imagine this approach has failed completely - why? This isn't adversarial critique (that's for decisions), it's assumption excavation.

### Phase 3: PERSPECTIVES
Socratic + collaborative reasoning. "How would others see this?"

Force yourself out of your own head. Different viewpoints, simulated stakeholders. You're not seeking answers yet - you're collecting different ways of seeing.

### Phase 4: EDGES
Creative + analogical reasoning. "What's at the boundaries?"

Go to the weird places. Edge cases, extremes, analogies from other domains. Finding unexpected connections and "what if?" scenarios. Hold contradictions, don't resolve them.

### Phase 5: META
Substrate observation. "What am I avoiding looking at?"

Step back and observe the exploration itself. What patterns emerged? What kept coming up? What was I avoiding? Surfaces blind spots that other phases might have reinforced.

### Phase 6: HARVEST
Questions catalog + hypothesis board. "What did we discover?"

NOT deciding, but crystallizing what was learned. Output is:
- Interesting questions that emerged
- Constraints discovered
- Hypotheses worth testing
- Updated systems map showing what changed

## Cognition-MCP Operations per Phase

1. **MAP**: `systems` + `visual_reasoning`
2. **INVERT**: `mental_model` (pre-mortem, inversion, assumption-surfacing)
3. **PERSPECTIVES**: `collaborative_reasoning` + `socratic_method`
4. **EDGES**: `creative_thinking` + `analogical_reasoning`
5. **META**: `meta` (substrate observation mode)
6. **HARVEST**: Structured `thought` with visual output

## Output Characteristics

The output is NOT a decision or plan. It's a richer understanding of the problem space that can feed into `/planthink` when ready.

Valid outputs include:
- "I'm more confused now but in useful ways"
- "These 5 questions are more important than the one I started with"
- "I discovered constraints I didn't know existed"
- "Here are 3 hypotheses worth testing"
